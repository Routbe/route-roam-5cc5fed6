# What does a badge say?

A badge tells you something specific. Not everything about a person, not everything about a company, and never that you should trust someone blindly.

## Four signals, four meanings

Don't just look at the color. The meaning and the source go hand in hand. ROUT distinguishes between a human link, public identity verification, creator status, and business verification.

### White seal: linked to a human

<BadgePreview type="human" />

An alias can be linked to a verified primary account without revealing the legal name on that alias. The human seal shows that connection. The current ROUT display uses a hand with a seal; the term “white shield” is also used for this signal.

This is not proof that all posts are accurate, not a reputation score, and not a replacement for a separate authorization check.

### Blue checkmark: public identity

<BadgePreview type="verified" />

The blue identity checkmark belongs to the verified primary profile. It is different from verifying that someone owns a social media account. Read the verification explanation on the profile to understand which identity was verified.

A blue checkmark does not grant a professional license, financial guarantee, or endorsement by ROUT.

### Pink checkmark: creator / influencer

<BadgePreview type="creator" />

Creator status is a separate, application-managed signal. Adding a pink icon to a profile picture yourself does not grant that status. Linked social accounts and any approvals are verified outside the public profile view.

The badge is not a quality guarantee for content, reach, or collaborations. Follower counts can change, and cached numbers are not a live measurement.

### Black checkmark: verified business

<BadgePreview type="business" />

Business verification makes the business identity recognizable. The signal is not a general endorsement of products, not insurance, and not proof that someone is authorized to perform every action on behalf of that company.

For sensitive transactions, always check the domain, context, and the contact person's authorization.

## Official is not the same as issued by an app

A custom badge can represent a club, family, organization, or other issuer. A diploma or certificate is an assertion by that issuer. It does not automatically carry the meaning of a ROUT identity badge.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the issuer">
A title and a nice icon do not prove accreditation. Look at the issuer, the date, the described criteria, and any revocation. A visual badge is not automatically a cryptographically verifiable credential.
</Callout>

The existing custom badge display opens details when you click on it. Broad external issuance of diplomas and a complete acceptance flow have not yet been confirmed as a working service. Therefore, this guide makes no promises about an API or visibility toggle that hasn't been verified live.

Read more at [Custom badges](/info/custom-badges) and [Credentials](/info/credentials).

## How to evaluate a profile

<StepByStep>
<Step title="Read the meaning">Open the badge explanation. A name check, human link, and account ownership are completely different checks.</Step>
<Step title="Check the source">With a custom badge, the origin must be clear. A name that resembles an official authority is not proof of an actual relationship.</Step>
<Step title="Check the context">Do not use a badge as a replacement for a contract, diploma validation, age verification, or payment security.</Step>
</StepByStep>

## If something isn't right

A badge that has been revoked, rejected, or not chosen for publication should not be treated as a valid public credential. The credential extension has separate fields for this; its full public enforcement must be part of the implementation, not just the documentation.

Notice a suspicious signal? Save the public profile URL and report the specific discrepancy via [Contact](/contact). Do not share identity documents, access tokens, or private keys in a report.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I give myself an official checkmark?

No. A profile field, image, or custom badge cannot grant official verification status. That status is verified separately.

### Why doesn't my alias show the same name as my primary profile?

An alias may be intended specifically to keep your legal name private. A human link can be visible without publishing that name.

### Are badges valid forever?

No. Status and context can change. Revocation, account modifications, and new checks can affect a signal. Check the current explanation instead of an old screenshot.

### Does a badge prove that a person is unique?

Not every badge does. OIDC pairwise subjects and Sybil nullifiers are technical identity mechanisms, not a meaning you can attribute to every visible icon.
